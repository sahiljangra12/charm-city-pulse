            About & methods

            What this is

            Charm City Pulse brings Baltimore's live environmental conditions and neighborhood sustainability data into one place. Resident mode answers "is it safe outside today, and how does my block compare?" Researcher mode opens every indicator for mapping, comparison, correlation and download.

            Built by Sahil (MSE Data Science, Johns Hopkins University) on two of his research projects: the Baltimore Heat Atlas and the Baltimore Street Trees Cooling study.

            Report-card scores

            Each theme score is the average city percentile of its inputs across Baltimore's residential census tracts, oriented so 100 = most burdened. A score of 80 means the tract is more burdened than about 80% of tracts.

              A ⚠ appears when an input's estimate is unreliable (coefficient of variation above 30%). Health measures are CDC model-based estimates, not counts of diagnosed people. Neighborhood (CSA) shares are recomputed from pooled tract counts, and neighborhood median income is interpolated from pooled income brackets.

              How it stays current

              • Live (in your browser): weather, heat index and wind chill, air quality (EPA monitors + model), NWS alerts, USGS stream gauges, FEMA flood zones, OpenStreetMap amenities.
              • Daily build: a scheduled job pulls the newest ACS 5-year release, the newest CDC PLACES release, the city's cooling-center, park, pool and rec-center layers, and geocodes shelter addresses. If a source is down, the last good copy is kept.
              • Research layers: summer surface temperature, land cover and the heat vulnerability index come from the Heat Atlas and are updated when the study is re-run.

              Limitations

              • Satellite surface temperature is not the air temperature people feel, though the two track closely in summer.
              • Weather and modeled air quality are for central Baltimore; conditions vary block to block. Official monitor readings are shown when available.
              • FEMA flood zones are regulatory maps and miss much street and basement (rain-driven) flooding.
              • Cooling and warming centers open only when the city activates them; call 311 to confirm.
              • OpenStreetMap features are community-mapped and may be incomplete.
              • Not a substitute for official alerts. In an emergency call 911.
