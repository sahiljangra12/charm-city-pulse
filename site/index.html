          About

          What this is

          Charm City Pulse answers two everyday questions for Baltimore: is it safe to be outside right now, and how does my block compare, along with where to go for help. Researcher mode opens every underlying number for mapping, comparison and download.

          Built by Sahil (MSE Data Science, Johns Hopkins University) on two of his studies: the Baltimore Heat Atlas and Do new street trees cool Baltimore?

          How the comparisons work

          Each area is compared with Baltimore's other residential census tracts. "Higher than most" means the area ranks in the top fifth of the city on that topic; "about average" means the middle fifth.

            Health figures are CDC model-based estimates, not counts of diagnosed people. Census figures are survey estimates and carry a margin of error, shown in researcher mode.

            How it stays current

            • Live, in your browser: weather, heat index and wind chill, air quality (EPA monitors and a forecast model), NWS alerts, stream gauges and FEMA flood zones.
            • Every morning: the newest Census and CDC releases, city cooling centers, parks, pools and rec centers, and OpenStreetMap places such as fountains, bike lanes and markets.
            • From the research: summer heat, tree cover and the heat vulnerability index.

            Limits

            • Weather and modeled air quality are for central Baltimore; conditions vary block to block.
            • FEMA flood zones miss much street and basement flooding from heavy rain.
            • Cooling and warming centers open only when the city activates them; call 311 to confirm.
            • Not a substitute for official alerts. In an emergency, call 911.
            Charm City Pulse uses public data from the U.S. Census Bureau, CDC, EPA, NOAA's National Weather Service, USGS, FEMA, Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap and Baltimore City. References · About · Privacy ·
