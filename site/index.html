            About

            Charm City Pulse turns public data into everyday answers for people who live in Baltimore.

            What it does

            It answers three everyday questions: is it safe to be outside right now, when is the best time for my plans, and how does my block compare, along with where to go for help. The Data section opens every number behind those answers.

            Built by Sahil (MSE Data Science, Johns Hopkins University) on two of his studies: the Baltimore Heat Atlas and Do new street trees cool Baltimore?

            How the comparisons work

            Each area is compared with Baltimore's other residential census tracts. "Hotter than most" means it ranks in the hottest two-fifths of the city; "among the hottest" means the top fifth.

              Health figures are CDC model-based estimates. Census figures are survey estimates with a margin of error, shown in My block under "See the numbers".

              How it stays current

              • Live in your browser: weather, heat index and wind chill, air quality from EPA monitors and a forecast model, NWS alerts, stream gauges and FEMA flood zones.
              • Every morning: the newest Census and CDC releases, city cooling centers, parks, pools and rec centers, and OpenStreetMap places.
              • From the research: summer heat, tree cover and the heat vulnerability index.

              Limits

              • Weather and modeled air quality are for central Baltimore; conditions vary block to block.
              • FEMA flood zones miss much of the street and basement flooding from heavy rain.
              • Cooling and warming centers open only when the city activates them. Call 311 to confirm.
              • Not a substitute for official alerts. In an emergency, call 911.
